About the archive

Curious, not gullible.

A place to enjoy the world's strangest stories and come away knowing what's actually true. A good mystery is more fun when you can tell the clues from the noise.

The ground rules

  • Facts come first. Dates, names and numbers stick to well-documented sources.
  • Speculation is labelled. Every case file has a "What we know" column and a "What's speculation" column, and theories never sneak into the first one.
  • Uncertainty is said out loud. When historians or scientists disagree, the story says so.
  • Sources are linked. Every file ends with Wikipedia plus reputable further reading, so you can dig deeper yourself.
  • Real photographs, credited. Images come from Wikimedia Commons: public domain or free licences, with the credit and licence on every one.

The four stamps

Rabbit hole levels

How deep a story goes: how much evidence, how many theories, and how long you could spend reading about it.

    Clearance ranks

    Reading files, finishing quizzes and earning seals raises your clearance, from Unclassified all the way to Eyes Only. See yours on your library card.

    Spotted a mistake?

    History gets revised and new evidence turns up. Every story lives in one file, data/stories.json, and details still waiting for a fact-check are flagged there. Add &review=1 to any story's address to see them highlighted.
